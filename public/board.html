    Hits start small

    Every game on the board is already bigger than Grow a Garden was at its buy-in. Grow a Garden had about 500 to 1,000 players at two to three weeks old when Splitting Point bought into it. The creator kept 50%.

    The gap opens fast after that. Grow a Garden reached about 100,000 players by day 32. Steal An Egg had 371,000 on day 27. No game on the board is near that pace, so price them on the money they make now.

    Most hits also crash. Grow a Garden 2 opened with 400,000 players and is down to about 2,000. Kick a Lucky Block fell from 1.7 million to about 7,000. In our method, how fast a game fades sets its price.

    How fast it fades sets the price

    If a game loses a share of its players every month, its future revenue adds up to about one month's revenue divided by that share. Analyst David Taylor says short-lived games sell for one to two months of revenue and durable ones for about 12, so we cap it at 12. These past games line up with the few deal prices that have been reported.

    The decline rates are our estimates from reported player counts, and the fast-fade rows start from event peaks, so they overstate the drop. A game under six weeks old, or still rising, has no fade to measure yet. We price fast-rise games at 1 to 2 months and slow-build games at 3 to 6. Upside is better bought with a stake or revenue share than with a bigger upfront price.

        What moves a game up its range

        Buyers pay for staying power. Voldex has said it buys established communities because marketing can't cheaply rebuild them, and it moved Brookhaven to weekly updates.

        • Each update leaves a higher low point than the last
        • Players come back on many days, not one long session
        • Ownership sits in a group, with IP assignments from every contributor
        • No borrowed characters, brands or music
        • Kids and Select audiences already unlocked
        • Most traffic comes from Roblox recommendations, not ads

        A starting range, not an appraisal. Everything stays in your browser. Robux convert at the DevEx rate, $0.0038 each. Visits convert at $0.004 to $0.012 of developer revenue each. We cap the price at 12 months of revenue even for durable games, because buyers discount for risk.

        How a sale works

        Roblox has allowed official ownership transfers since December 2024. What changes hands is the game and the right to earn Robux from it. Robux already earned stays with the seller. This is general information, not legal advice, and we don't take part in sales.

        1. Before anything moves

          First, put it in writing. A purchase agreement, plus written IP assignments from everyone who built the game. Without a company, co-developers may count as partners.

        2. Before the transfer

          Then fund escrow. The buyer pays a licensed escrow service, timed to the transfer. Nobody sends money or the game first.

        3. Up to 7 days

          Then start the transfer. The owner starts Roblox's ownership transfer to the buyer from the game's settings. The request expires after 7 days.

        4. Up to 24 hours

          Expect a short outage. Servers close while the game moves, and discovery can dip for up to 24 hours. Private modules and packages have to be re-uploaded.

        5. Then 30 days locked

          Last, escrow releases. Payment goes out once the transfer shows complete. The new owner can't transfer it again for 30 days.

        If the seller is under 18, a parent or guardian should sign too, and both sides should get legal advice first. In California, a minor can generally cancel a contract they signed.

        How we score and price

        Score weights
        Momentum25Latest visits against the lifetime average, players over the past week, and Up-and-Coming rank. Being on Top Trending earns nothing, because our track record showed it usually means a game is near its peak
        Stickiness25Minutes per visit against GameAnalytics benchmarks (median 6.6, top quarter 14.5, top tenth 26.4), rating, favorites and votes per 1,000 visits, Top Revisited
        Updates15How recently it updated, and whether it's still shipping after a month
        Originality15Slow-build genres score higher. Games built on a current trend lose points, because Roblox says it no longer prioritizes close look-alikes in recommendations
        Earnings10Rank on Top Earning, the main public sign of revenue
        Ease of buying10Group or solo ownership, verified badge, group size, names that reference other brands

        We read Roblox's public chart lists and pulled each game's public stats.

        For the games that make the board, we also read their developer products, live servers, the creator's other games, the content maturity rating and badges, all from Roblox's public data. Rank changes and days on the board compare each day's board with the one before.

        Minutes per visit is players × 1,440 ÷ visits a day.

        Momentum and stickiness are ranked against the rest of today's pool. Each unusual-number flag takes off 5 points and moves the game to the check-first list. The weights are our judgment, not a tested model.

        Revenue a month = visits a month × 1–3% who pay × 150 Robux × 70% creator share × $0.0038 per Robux, or about $0.004 to $0.012 per visit

        The price we show is middle revenue × middle months, marked low confidence. The full range in each game's details runs from low revenue × low months to high revenue × high months, a 6× spread that reflects how little you can see from outside a game. Once we've tracked a game for 4 weeks, the months come from its measured decline instead of its genre. Rebuild the price on real revenue once the seller shares it. A 2026 game probably earns less per player than the 2025 hits did.
